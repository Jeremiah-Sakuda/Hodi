How it works

Declare. Decide. Enforce.

01
Declare
You register a claim about your work — title, medium, a SHA-256 fingerprint computed in your browser. The registry holds claims, never masters: your file never leaves your device. Then you declare terms: which uses may be licensed, commercial or not, attribution or not.
02
Decide
A buyer asks in plain language. Gemini 3.5 on Vertex AI interprets the sentence into typed terms — use, model class, commerce, territory, attribution. Then deterministic policy code decides against your declaration. The model interprets; it never decides.
03
Enforce
Every yes is an event in an append-only log. Revocation cascades through everything your yes contained — derived from a scope lattice, not hard-coded — is signed by Cloud KMS, and closes the standing offer. History is struck through, never erased.
Why a fleet, not a chatbot

Five agents, split by conflict of interest.

The agent that negotiates licenses must never touch revocations. Each agent runs under its own Google Cloud service-account identity with its own database permissions, and every call crosses an agent gateway that enforces policy structurally — not a prompt asking nicely. When a worker hangs, a supervisor quarantines it and reroutes the task. Run the fleet live in the walkthrough ↗

What Hodi refuses to claim

A registry that flatters you is worthless.

✕That your work is inside a model’s training data. It cannot reliably know — so there is no field for the claim, anywhere in the schema.
✕That revocation pulls your work out of a model. It withdraws permission going forward and closes the offer. It does not un-train anything, and says so on every notice.
✕That your ownership is verified before you prove it. Unproven claims are labeled asserted, in public, including the author’s own.
